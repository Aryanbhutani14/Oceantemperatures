01

Satellites see the surface. The ocean lives below it.

Cyclones, the monsoon and fisheries all depend on how much heat sits in the upper few hundred metres of the North Indian Ocean, especially in the thermocline between 50 and 200 m, where temperature drops fastest.

Satellites measure temperature, salinity, sea level and winds at the surface every day, everywhere. Below the surface we only have ARGO floats: accurate, but a few thousand profiles a year for an entire basin.

We ask a simple question: how much of the subsurface can be recovered from the surface alone?

The gap

Only 4,071 ARGO profiles fell in our region in 2021, against 4.3 million ocean cell-days on a 0.25° grid.

What the surface knows

Warm water expands, so a thick warm layer lifts sea level by a few centimetres. Winds drive mixing and upwelling. These signals carry information about the column below.

Our approach

Course machine learning models, one per depth, trained on GLORYS reanalysis with a strict month-based split, and judged against climatology and independent ARGO floats.

02

From satellite pixels to a full column.

Train on reanalysis. Predict from satellites only. Validate on floats the model never saw.

  1. 01

    Acquire

    Eight datasets for 2021: SST, salinity, sea level, currents, winds, reanalysis, climatology, ARGO and cyclone tracks.

    CMEMS · ARGO GDAC · IBTrACS
  2. 02

    Harmonise

    One area-weighted regridder puts every source on the same 0.25° daily grid; 15 standard depths; ocean mask of 11,880 cells.

    xarray · numpy
  3. 03

    Explore

    Sea level tracks 100 m temperature (ρ 0.71); extreme SST values are real cyclone cold wakes, so they are kept.

    EDA · PCA
  4. 04

    Engineer

    40 features: wind stress, Ekman pumping, gradients, 7-day memory, location, season and a PCA summary of the local surface pattern.

    40 features
  5. 05

    Model

    13 regression models compared on identical test rows; chosen only by cross-validation that holds out whole months.

    scikit-learn
  6. 06

    Validate

    Four unseen test months, independent ARGO floats, and indicators forecasters use: D20, TCHP, mixed layer depth.

    ARGO · TEOS-10

Test months February, May, August and November (one per season), with seven-day gaps around each so that neighbouring days never sit on both sides of the split.

The heat that feeds cyclones.

07

Every model, the same test.

Thirteen regression models from linear regression to boosting, plus two baselines. All tuned by GroupKFold cross-validation that holds out whole months, and all scored on the same 200,000 test rows.

Will the ocean feed a storm?

A yes/no version of the problem: is TCHP at least 60 kJ/cm²? Seven classifiers were tuned by cross-validated ROC-AUC; logistic regression with an L1 penalty was selected. Move the threshold and watch the trade-off.

09

Why it matters.

A fast, cheap estimate of the ocean column from data that arrives every day.

01

Cyclone preparedness

Daily TCHP and D26 maps from satellites, without waiting for the next float to surface.

02

Fisheries

A shallow thermocline marks upwelling of cold, nutrient-rich water, where fish gather.

03

Monsoon and climate

Upper-ocean heat content links to monsoon behaviour over the subcontinent.

04

Maritime operations

Sound travels differently through layers of different temperature, which matters for acoustics and navigation.
